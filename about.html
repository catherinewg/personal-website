Philosophy

My philosophy rests
on three pillars.

They decide what gets built, what gets cut, and which conversations happen early instead of late.

01

Become customer obsessed

Not customer-informed, not customer-adjacent — obsessed. The customer's actual behaviour, what they clicked and what they abandoned and what they said when nobody was selling to them, outranks internal consensus every single time. Most brand disagreements are settled by going and looking.

02

Fail fast and learn

Shipping is how you find out. I'd rather run the campaign, read the data and be wrong in three weeks than be theoretically correct in a deck for six months. The point of instrumenting everything is that being wrong becomes cheap and fast, which is the only condition under which people take real swings.

03

Practice radical candor

Care personally, challenge directly. The most expensive thing in a marketing team is the objection nobody raised — the doubt that stayed polite until the launch. Warmth is what makes the hard conversation survivable, which is exactly why warmth and directness belong together rather than in tension.

Process

How it goes.

I

Instrument

Before opinions, visibility. If we can't see who's arriving and what they do, every later decision is a guess wearing a deck.

II

Position

One page. If the position needs more than a page it isn't one yet. This is the part we should argue about.

III

Build

Campaigns, editorial system, film, lifecycle — whatever the position actually requires, scoped after it's agreed rather than before.

IV

Review, retroactively

A standing cadence for reading the data and iterating against it. Instrumentation without a review habit is a dashboard nobody opens.
